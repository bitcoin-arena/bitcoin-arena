Bitcoin Arena developer docs

DecentralizedNon-custodialSettles in Bitcoin

Bitcoin Arena is an engine for games and prediction markets played for bitcoin. It has no central server and no company holding money: a federation of independent nodes takes the stakes, decides every result and pays the winners. This page explains how that works, every request a node answers, and how to build your own game or market on it.

Bitcoin Arena runs on Bitcoin testnet4 and on private test chains today. Everything here uses test coins. Mainnet is not live.

How Bitcoin Arena works

A game or market is published by its developer: a signed record of its rules.
Nodes choose which games they host and open rounds of them.
A player joins a round and stakes sats over Lightning. The stake is locked by the federation, not by any one party.
The nodes decide the result: they replay every game run, or check the signed outcome of a market.
The federation pays the winners, the nodes and the donations, exactly as the rules say.

Decentralized

There is no server to switch off. Any node can host any game, and a committee of nodes decides each round.

Non-custodial

No company, developer or single node holds players' sats. Stakes sit in locks of a federation (Fedimint) and move only as the round's result says.

Checked, not trusted

Game runs are replayed by independent nodes from the player's inputs. A run that does not replay to the same score is not paid.

No approval

A game exists once its developer signs it. Nobody has to accept it; node operators decide what they host.

Cheating is caught

A node that signs a false result leaves a proof anyone can check. The result is overturned, and the proof costs the node its bond. When a node cheats

When a node cheats

A node is not trusted. Everything it signs can be checked by someone else, and a false signature is evidence against it. This is what happens when one tries.

What a node could try

Each of these is a cheat, and each leaves a proof anyone can check:

The cheatThe proof
Signing two different results for the same player in one roundThe two signed results
Signing a result that replaying the player's inputs does not giveThe inputs, the nodes' receipts for them, and the replay
Signing a payout table that is not what the rules give for these entries and resultsThe table and its inputs
Paying out without the required 5 of 7 result and payout signaturesThe settlement record
Signing for a round its own operator played inThe player's entry and the node's signature
Claiming a fee share for work it did not doThe receipt record
Agreeing to a round's payout and then not signing it before the locks run outIts signed agreement, the transaction and the expiry

Why one node alone cannot do it

Every round has a committee of 7 seats, and a result or payout counts only when 5 of them sign the same one. Up to 9 nodes are drawn per role, so spares take the seats of nodes that do not show up. The draw comes from a Bitcoin block hash, and your app computes the committee itself. A cheating node is one signature of the five needed. To change a result, five nodes would have to lie together.

How it is caught: replay and the objection window

After a result is signed, it waits 10 minutes before it is final. In that time anyone can object: the player, another player, a node that is not in the committee, a spectator or a watchtower. An objection is not an opinion. It is the player's inputs, the receipts the committee's nodes signed for them, and the result that replaying them gives. Any node checks it the same way, by running the game again with the round's own rules (POST /objection; tools/check-objection.mjs does the same check outside any node).

  • A valid objection stops the result. The whole committee replays the inputs again, and the replay decides. The new result gets its own 10-minute window.
  • If the committee cannot get 5 of 7 to agree, the entry is void and the stake goes back to the player.
  • An objection that does not check is refused with its reason, and nothing is stored.

The player's app sends the inputs of the run to all the round's nodes as it plays, each node signs a receipt for them, so the evidence does not live only on the player's phone.

What evidence is kept

Nodes keep every proof they hold and hand it to anyone who asks (/slash-proofs, /equivocation). A proof has no deadline. An objection that arrives after the 10 minutes can no longer change a final result: the payment goes ahead. But it is still a proof against every node that signed the false result.

What happens to the cheater

Every node puts up a bond on Bitcoin to get a seat. The bond is designed with a slash path that only a separate set of arbitration keys can use, and only with a proof:

  • The arbitration keys sign a transaction that takes the bond. It names the proof's hash on chain, so anyone can fetch the proof later and check it again. A slash without a valid proof is itself public evidence against the arbitration set.
  • Half the bond goes to the players the cheat harmed. Of the other half, 75% goes evenly to the other active nodes (not the round's own seats) and 25% to the developers. Nothing is burned.
  • A spent bond is no longer a bond, so the node drops out of the census and out of every future committee. It would need a new bond to come back.

Arbitration touches bonds only. It can never move a player's stake, delay a round or change a result.

What the other nodes and your app do

  • Honest nodes replay every run themselves and sign only what their own replay gives. They will not co-sign a false result. They check and store objections, and replay again when one is valid.
  • Your app works out the committee and checks every signature itself, and sends your inputs to every node of the round as you play.

The honest limits

Bitcoin itself cannot replay a game, so a slash needs the arbitration set to confirm that the proof checks. That trust is limited, not removed: the arbitration set is separate from the committees, cannot touch stakes, and cannot hold a bond forever. In the design, 30 days after a bond confirms its owner can always take it back.

Your money never depends on slashing. Three things protect it even if arbitration failed entirely: the 5-of-7 rule, stakes that go back to you by default if nothing is signed, and the objection window. Slashing is the penalty for getting caught, not the thing that keeps you safe.

Today, on testnet4: objections, the re-run, void-and-refund and the evidence nodes keep are built and running. The slash itself is not: a node only works out and records what a slash would owe (/slash-owing), and no bond is taken. Mainnet is not live.

The words used here

  • Round: one match of a game or one market, with a start, an entry close and a stake. Every round has a 32-byte round id.
  • Entry: a player's place in a round, with their stake.
  • Node (also signer node): one running arena-node server. Its operator chooses the games it hosts.
  • Committee: the nodes drawn for one round. They run it and sign its results and payouts.
  • Federation and guardians: the Fedimint federation that holds the stakes; its guardians are nodes that each hold one of its keys.
  • Manifest: the developer's signed record of a game or market: its rules, its client files, how it pays out. Its hash is the game id.
  • Replayed game: the result is a run the nodes replay. Attested game (a prediction market): the result is an outcome signed by the people or services the developer named (its attestors).
  • Head-to-head game: each player hides a move, all moves are revealed after entry close, and the game's rule decides between them (Rock Paper Scissors). A round is played only when all its players entered.

What a node does

A node is a small Node.js server. Together the nodes are Bitcoin Arena; there is no other server. A node:

  • lists the games it hosts and the rounds it holds;
  • takes players' entries and the proof that their stake is locked;
  • replays the runs of a replayed game, or checks the attestation of a market;
  • signs results and the payout table with the other nodes of the committee;
  • as a guardian, holds one key of the federation that keeps the stakes.

A node is checked by the others in everything it signs. See When a node cheats.

For that work the nodes of a round share the round's node fee (5% of every paid stake today). It is split three ways: the nodes that replay the runs, the nodes that sign the payouts, and the guardians that hold the money. A node operator gets a share of the fees of every round their node serves.

Run a node

You need a computer that is always on with a public HTTPS address (a tunnel works), Node.js 24, the Bitcoin Arena source, and a Bitcoin node (bitcoind) of the same network, testnet4 today, so your node reads the chain itself. For the guardian role you also need fedimintd and a seat in the federation.

# start a node on port 8080 with its database in round.db
node packages/arena-node/src/main.js 8080 round.db

# the same, with the first game (Tick Tock, Next Block) hosted
node packages/nextblock/bin/node.mjs 8080 round.db

The node API

Every node answers the same HTTP requests at its own address, for example https://your-node.example/games. There are {{NALL}} in all. Requests are open to any page (CORS is open); the few that change something are protected by a signature, listed under Access.

  • Round ids, entry ids and keys are hex strings in the query: ?round=<64 hex>.
  • Answers are JSON, or binary when they are a protocol record (a round contract, a manifest, an attestation). Binary records are encoded and decoded with packages/arena-protocol.
  • Ask several nodes, not one: every node of the committee answers for itself, and the page compares them.

Implement a game

A game is four things: its rules (a small core every node can run and get the same result), its client (the web page the player sees), its donation key (where your share of the donations goes) and its manifest (your signed record of all of it). The example below is a whole game called coin-count: each tick the player presses a number, and the score is the sum.

  1. Write the rules as pure arithmetic

    Nodes on different machines must reach the same score bit for bit. So the core may use only integers (or fixed-point), the round's seed and the player's inputs: no clock, no Math.random, no network, no files. Every core has the same small interface:

    // core.js - coin-count
export const CORE = {
  name: 'coin-count',
  RUN_TICKS: 600,                                   // the longest run a round may ask
  runTicksFits: (t) => Number.isInteger(t) && t >= 1 && t <= 600,
  INPUT_WORDS: 5,                                   // a tick carries five input numbers
  start: (seedWord, config, { runTicks }) => ({ seed: seedWord | 0, sum: 0, ticks: 0, length: runTicks }),
  eventsOf: () => ({ count: { liquidations: 0, loads: 0, samples: 0, tapes: 0 }, before() {} }),
  step(st, words) { if (st.ticks < st.length) { st.sum += words[0] | 0; st.ticks++; } },
  stateHash: (st) => sha256(`coin-count:${st.seed}:${st.sum}:${st.ticks}:${st.length}`),
  result: (st) => ({ outcome: st.ticks < st.length ? null : 'DELIVERED', score: st.sum }),
};

    The core's id is the SHA-256 of its file. A config (a set of rule numbers) is named by its hash too.

  2. Write the client: what the player sees

    Your client is a web page at games/<game name>/index.html beside the Bitcoin Arena page. The page runs it only if its SHA-256 is in your manifest, so a client changed by one byte is refused. It gets the engine as window.parent.ARENA_GAME; stakes, locks, the wallet and the seed stay the engine's, so your client never touches a key.

    const A = window.parent.ARENA_GAME;
A.rows();          // this game's open rounds: id, line, joinable, entry close
A.identity();      // the player's name and key fingerprint (the same in every game)
A.join(roundId);   // join, through the engine's own join and stake
A.back();          // back to the list of games
  3. Make your donation key: where your share is paid 
    node packages/arena-donor/bin/arena-donor.mjs init ./my-game-dev --label "coin-count dev"
# prints recipientPk: the key your game's donation set names

    The same tool later collects your donations from the federation and pays them to your own Lightning node.

  4. Make your developer key 
    node packages/arena-donor/bin/arena-donor.mjs game-key ./my-game-dev
# writes ./my-game-dev/game-dev.pkcs8 (readable only by you) and prints your public key

    This Ed25519 key signs your game and every later version of it. Keep it safe; a node never sees it.

  5. Write the manifest 
    // body.mjs - coin-count, version 1
import { RESULT_TYPE, PAYOUT_RULE, NO_PARENT } from './packages/arena-protocol/src/manifest.js';
import { donationSetId } from './packages/arena-protocol/src/donation.js';
const hex = (h) => Uint8Array.from(Buffer.from(h, 'hex'));
export const DONATION_SET = [{ recipientPk: hex('02…your recipientPk…'), label: 'coin-count dev', shareBps: 10000 }];
export const BODY = {
  gameName: 'coin-count', version: 1, parentManifestId: NO_PARENT,
  resultType: RESULT_TYPE.REPLAY, payoutRule: PAYOUT_RULE.RANK_CURVE, rankNumerator: 3, rankDenominator: 4,
  minScore: 1, runTicksMax: 600, roundPeriodMs: 0n,
  cores: [{ hash: hex('…sha256 of core.js…'), configs: [hex('…sha256 of your config…')] }],
  clients: [hex('…sha256 of games/coin-count/index.html…')],
  gameDonationSetId: donationSetId(DONATION_SET),
};
  6. Sign it 
    node packages/arena-donor/bin/arena-donor.mjs sign ./my-game-dev body.mjs
# {"step":"signed","game":"coin-count","version":1,"gameId":"…","file":"./my-game-dev/coin-count-manifest.hex"}

    That id is your game. Change one byte of the manifest and the id changes too.

  7. Hand it to the nodes

    A node loads a game from a module that exports GAME, listed in its ARENA_GAMES. It checks every piece against the manifest at start and refuses a game that does not match.

    // coin-count/game.js
export const GAME = { name: 'coin-count', title: 'Coin Count', manifest, gameId,
  cores: new Map([[CORE_HASH, { name: 'coin-count', hash: CORE_HASH, url: CORE_URL, adapter: CORE,
    configs: new Map([[CONFIG_HASH, {}]]) }]]),
  donationSet: DONATION_SET };

    Then ask node operators to host it. Each decides alone; the game appears in the lobby of every page whose nodes host it. Check it with GET /games on their nodes.

  8. Test on your own machine

    packages/arena-node/tools/games-regtest.mjs starts a private test chain with two nodes hosting two games and serves the page, so you see the game picker, your client and a join with test coins.

  9. Ship updates

    An update is a new manifest with the same key, version + 1 and the old game id as parentManifestId. Rounds already written keep the manifest they named.

Paid rounds of a replayed game need the game on the page's list of known games (with its cores and configs), because that list ships with the page. A newly signed game plays free rounds first.

Implement a head-to-head game

In a replayed game every run is checked on its own, so one player's score can never depend on what another player did. A head-to-head game is for the games where it does: each player picks a move in secret, all moves are shown together, and the game's rule decides who won. The engine does the hiding, the showing, the checking and the money; your game only says what beats what. The example is the real Rock Paper Scissors (packages/rock-paper-scissors).

Result typeThe result comes fromExample
REPLAY (1)each player's own run, replayed by every nodeTick Tock, Next Block
ATTESTED (2)an outcome signed by the attestors the game namesa prediction market
HEAD_TO_HEAD (3)all players' revealed moves together, decided by the game's ruleRock Paper Scissors

A round, step by step

  1. Hide. Before entry close the player enters with a commitment instead of a move: H(round id ‖ session key ‖ move ‖ salt), with a fresh 32-byte salt. Nobody can read the move from it, and nobody can copy it: it names the entry's own session key.
  2. Wait for the other players. A round seats exactly the game's players. A further entry is refused ("that round already seats … 2 players").
  3. Reveal. Between entry close and the round's end, each player sends its move and salt to every node (POST /reveal). A node takes it only if it hashes to that entry's commitment.
  4. Decide. At the round's end every node pulls the other nodes' reveals (GET /reveals), hands all the moves to the game's rule, and signs each player's result, exactly as it signs a replayed run. Same moves, same rule, so every node signs the same result.
  5. Pay. The result goes through the normal settlement, unchanged:
What happenedResult the nodes signMoney
You wonDELIVERED, score 2your stake back plus the losers' stakes
A drawDELIVERED, score 1everyone keeps their own stake
You lostFAILEDyour stake goes to the winner
You did not revealFORFEITyour stake goes to the winner (a lone revealer wins)
Fewer players than the game needs enteredVOID for everyonenot played: every stake comes back

Build it

  1. Write the rule

    One function: it gets the moves of the players who revealed and says WIN, DRAW or LOSS for each. Like a core it must be pure: same moves, same answer, on every machine.

    // plugin/decide.js - Rock Paper Scissors: 0 rock, 1 paper, 2 scissors
export const DECIDE = {
  name: 'rock-paper-scissors decide v1',
  decide(moves) {                                   // [{ key, move }]  ->  { [key]: 'WIN' | 'DRAW' | 'LOSS' }
    const shown = [...new Set(moves.map((m) => m.move))];
    const out = {};
    if (shown.length !== 2) { for (const m of moves) out[m.key] = 'DRAW'; return out; }
    const beats = (a, b) => (a - b + 3) % 3 === 1;
    const winning = beats(shown[0], shown[1]) ? shown[0] : shown[1];
    for (const m of moves) out[m.key] = m.move === winning ? 'WIN' : 'LOSS';
    return out;
  },
};

    Its id is the SHA-256 of its file, and the manifest names it in cores[], so a node installs and checks it just like a replay core.

  2. Write the manifest 
    export const BODY = {
  gameName: 'rock-paper-scissors', version: 1, parentManifestId: NO_PARENT,
  resultType: RESULT_TYPE.HEAD_TO_HEAD,
  cores: [{ hash: sha256(decide.js), configs: [sha256('rock-paper-scissors-config:v1')] }],
  runTicksMax: 0,                                   // no run
  payoutRule: PAYOUT_RULE.RANK_CURVE, rankNumerator: 3, rankDenominator: 4,
  minScore: 2,                                      // a win scores 2, a draw 1
  players: 2,                                       // 2 to 16: a round is played only with all of them
  moveCount: 3,                                     // 2 to 32 moves
  revealWindowMs: 120000n,                          // 10 s to 1 h: the longest a round may give for reveals
  roundPeriodMs: 0n, clients: [sha256(games/rock-paper-scissors/index.html)], gameDonationSetId,
};

    Sign it and hand it to the nodes exactly as in Implement a game (arena-donor sign, a module exporting GAME, listed in ARENA_GAMES).

  3. Write the client

    Besides rows, identity and back, a head-to-head client gets these from window.parent.ARENA_GAME. The salt, the stake and the keys stay the engine's.

    const A = window.parent.ARENA_GAME;
A.headToHead;          // { players, moveCount } - or null for another kind of game
A.hide(roundId, move); // enter the round with this move hidden
A.hidden(roundId);     // this tab's hidden move in that round (kept in the tab until revealed)
A.hiddenAll();         // every move this tab hid in this game - a round leaves the rows at its entry close
A.reveal(roundId);     // after entry close: send the move and salt to every node
A.reveals(roundId);    // the moves the nodes hold: { [entryId]: move }
A.result(roundId);     // the round as the nodes hold it: entries, each signed outcome and how many nodes signed it

    Show the players A.result, the signed result, not a verdict your page worked out itself. Rock Paper Scissors' client shows "waiting for an opponent (1 of 2)" before entry close, reveals at entry close, and then "You win - signed by 2 nodes".

  4. Keep it apart from other games

    A page shows one game's rounds at a time: on nodes that host Tick Tock, Next Block and Rock Paper Scissors, the truck's lobby lists only its own rounds, and Rock Paper Scissors loads none of the truck.

  5. Try it on your own machine 
    ARENA_PAGE=<a page with games/rock-paper-scissors/index.html> node packages/rock-paper-scissors/tools/demo-regtest.mjs ./work
# a private test chain, two nodes hosting both games, one free round always open; prints the lobby link

    Open the link twice in two browser profiles (two players) and add &game=rock-paper-scissors.

Implement a prediction market

A market is a round where players pick one outcome and the right picks share the stakes of the wrong ones. It uses the same custody, nodes and settlement as a game, but there is no core and no run: the result is the outcome reported by the attestors you name.

  1. Choose the question, the outcomes and who reports the result

    2 to 16 outcomes, for example ['up', 'down']. Then 1 to 16 attestors: Ed25519 keys of the people or services that report the outcome (for example three price-feed operators), and how many of them must sign the same outcome (for example 2 of 3). Players see who decides before they stake.

  2. Write the manifest

    The same as a game's, with these differences:

    export const BODY = {
  gameName: 'btc-up-down', version: 1, parentManifestId: NO_PARENT,
  resultType: RESULT_TYPE.ATTESTED,          // the result is an outcome, not a replayed run
  payoutRule: PAYOUT_RULE.PARI_MUTUEL,       // the right picks share the wrong picks' stakes
  outcomes: ['up', 'down'],
  attestors: [KEY_A, KEY_B, KEY_C],          // 32-byte Ed25519 public keys
  attestThreshold: 2,                        // 2 of the 3 must sign the same outcome
  cores: [], runTicksMax: 0,                 // nothing to run
  clients: [hex('…sha256 of games/btc-up-down/index.html…')],
  gameDonationSetId: donationSetId(DONATION_SET),
  // … roundPeriodMs and the rest as for a game
};

    Sign it and hand it to the nodes exactly as for a game (steps 4, 6 and 7 above). Your client shows the outcomes; the page's own pick sheet takes the stake and the pick.

  3. Players pick and stake

    An entry into a market round carries a pick, the index of one outcome. Nodes refuse a pick that is out of range, and any pick once they hold a valid result for that round.

  4. Report the outcome

    After the entry close, your attestors sign the round's outcome and anyone posts it to the nodes. What they sign is the id of { round id, outcome index, observed time in ms }:

    import { createPrivateKey, sign } from 'node:crypto';
import { attestationBodyId, encodeAttestation } from './packages/arena-protocol/src/attested.js';

const body = { roundId, outcome: 0, observedAtMs: BigInt(Date.now()) };   // 0 = 'up'
const digest = Buffer.from(attestationBodyId(body));
const signatures = [attestorA, attestorB].map((a) => ({
  key: a.publicKey,                                                     // 32 bytes
  signature: new Uint8Array(sign(null, digest, a.privateKey)),          // Ed25519, 64 bytes
}));
const attestation = encodeAttestation({ ...body, signatures });

for (const node of committeeNodes)
  await fetch(`${node}/outcome?round=${roundIdHex}`, { method: 'POST', body: attestation });

    Each node checks that the attestation is for this round, that the outcome exists, and that enough of the named attestors signed it. It answers why when it refuses.

  5. Follow it

    GET /outcome?round=… answers the round's state: OPEN (no result yet), ATTESTED (one outcome, with the payout table) or DISPUTED. The round settles once a result has stood for the objection window (600 s) with nothing contradicting it.

  6. If attestors contradict themselves

    Two valid attestations of different outcomes for one round are the proof that the attestors disagreed. The round is DISPUTED and pays out on neither outcome; the stakes go back to their players by the refund path.

How a market pays out (pari-mutuel)

  • A market's fees are its own, set in its manifest: 0.5% to the nodes and 0.5% donation by default, at most 5% each and 10% together. Its net stake in the pool is the stake less those fees: a 1 sat pick at 1% stakes 0.99 sat on the odds.
  • The fees are due when an order fills, for winners and losers alike; a pick fills at the round's entry close. Before that, an order can be cancelled at no cost: the player signs POST /cancel, and its stake and fee locks come back at once. The fees sit in the fee lock until the market resolves; then the nodes take their part and the donation is paid to the protocol developers' and the market developer's keys.
  • Every entry that picked the winning outcome gets its net stake back plus its share of the losing pool: losing pool × its net stake ÷ all winning net stakes, rounded down; the millisats left over go one each to the winners.
  • If nobody picked the winning outcome, or the market is void, everything goes back: every stake and every fee. A market is void if its attestors contradict each other, or if no outcome stands by its deadline.
  • There is no house and no counterparty: the wrong picks pay the right ones.

Bitcoin Up or Down, every 15 minutes

The first market follows Polymarket's BTC Up or Down 15m. There is one market per quarter hour with two outcomes, Up and Down. Up wins if the time-weighted average BTC price over the window is at or above the price at its start (the price to beat); a tie is Up. The attestors apply that rule to the prices they read (upDownOutcome in packages/arena-protocol/src/up-down.js) and sign the outcome.

Trading uses signed limit orders. Prices are in whole cents (0.01 to 0.99), an order buys at least 5 shares, and a share pays 1 sat if its side wins. An Up buyer at 0.73 and a Down buyer at 0.27 together fund a share. The nodes clear each batch of orders at one price that every node computes the same way (clearBatch in order-book.js). The fee is charged on the part of an order that filled, when it fills; what has not filled can be cancelled at no cost.

The example market btc-close (three outcomes, three attestors, 2 of 3) lives in packages/arena-node/test/plugins/market/ with its own client, and packages/arena-client/tools/market-live.sh runs it on a private test chain end to end.

Where the money goes

Every paid entry is split the same way, fixed by the protocol. A 10,000 sat stake with the default donation:

PartShareGoes to
In play90%the round: what is won or lost
Node fee5%the nodes that ran the round: replay, settlement, guardians, a third each
Donation, half2.5%the game's developer
Donation, half2.5%the protocol developers

Donations are split. The developer takes no fees, only donations. The donation is the player's choice: 5% by default, down to zero. Free rounds pay neither. A prediction market charges its own manifest's fees instead: 1% by default (0.5% nodes, 0.5% donation), due when it resolves (see Implement a prediction market).
